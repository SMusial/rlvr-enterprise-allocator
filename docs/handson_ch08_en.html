❓ What is Chapter 08?

Ch08 introduces Eligibility Traces — a more elegant alternative to n-Step TD. Instead of waiting n steps, traces decay all past (s,a) pairs simultaneously.

🧮 Eligibility Trace

$$e_t(s,a) = \gamma\lambda \cdot e_{t-1}(s,a) + \mathbf{1}[s=S_t, a=A_t]$$
Replacing traces: set to 1 on visit (instead of accumulating). Decay factor $\gamma\lambda = 0.665$ for $\gamma=0.95, \lambda=0.7$. Pruned at 1e-8. Implemented in ch08_eligibility.rs.

SARSA(λ) Update

$$\delta_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma Q(S', A') - Q(S, A)$$
$$Q(s,a) \leftarrow Q(s,a) + \alpha \delta_t e_t(s,a) \quad \forall(s,a)$$
Updates all (s,a) pairs simultaneously, weighted by their eligibility. High trace = recently visited = gets more credit. Implemented in sarsa_lambda().

Q(λ) Watkins

$$\delta_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(S', a') - Q(S, A)$$
TD target uses greedy max (off-policy). Watkins cut: traces set to 0 on non-greedy action. This prevents divergence but reduces propagation. Implemented in q_lambda().

Task 1 — Eligibility trace decay

State S3, action A1 was visited at step t=0. γ=0.95, λ=0.7. What is the trace value $e_t(S3,A1)$ at steps t=1, t=2, t=3 (assuming no revisit)?

💡 Show answer
Decay factor: $\gamma\lambda = 0.95 \times 0.7 = 0.665$
$$e_1(S3,A1) = 0.665 \cdot 1.0 = \mathbf{0.665}$$
$$e_2(S3,A1) = 0.665 \cdot 0.665 = \mathbf{0.442}$$
$$e_3(S3,A1) = 0.665 \cdot 0.442 = \mathbf{0.294}$$

Task 2 — SARSA(λ) update

$\delta_t = 3.5$. Two (s,a) pairs have active traces: $e(S2,A1)=0.8$, $e(S5,A0)=0.3$. α=0.1. Compute the Q-value updates for both pairs.

💡 Show answer
$$\Delta Q(S2,A1) = \alpha \cdot \delta_t \cdot e(S2,A1) = 0.1 \cdot 3.5 \cdot 0.8 = \mathbf{0.28}$$
$$\Delta Q(S5,A0) = \alpha \cdot \delta_t \cdot e(S5,A0) = 0.1 \cdot 3.5 \cdot 0.3 = \mathbf{0.105}$$
Both pairs are updated simultaneously — this is the key advantage of eligibility traces.

Task 3 — Watkins cut

Q(λ) agent is in state S3. The greedy action is A1, but ε-greedy chooses A0 (exploration). What happens to all eligibility traces?

💡 Show answer
When a non-greedy action is taken (A0 instead of greedy A1):
• All eligibility traces are set to 0 (Watkins cut)
• Only the current (S3,A0) gets a new trace of 1
• This prevents the off-policy update from propagating through on-policy traces

This is why Q(λ) has fewer active traces than SARSA(λ) — exploration steps cut all traces.

Task 4 — Why λ=0.7 is the sweet spot

Explain why λ=0 (TD0) and λ=0.99 (≈MC) are both suboptimal, and why λ=0.7 typically performs best.

💡 Show answer
λ=0 (TD0): Only the current (s,a) is updated. Slow credit assignment — reward signal propagates one step at a time. Many episodes needed.

λ=0.99 (≈MC): All recent (s,a) pairs get nearly equal credit. High variance — noisy updates. Requires small α for stability.

λ=0.7 sweet spot: Recent (s,a) pairs get significant credit (fast propagation) while distant ones get little (low variance). The decay $0.665^k$ drops to <0.1 after ~6 steps — a natural horizon.

Task 5 — Eligibility traces vs n-Step TD

Both eligibility traces and n-Step TD bridge TD(0) and MC. What is the key computational advantage of eligibility traces?

💡 Show answer
n-Step TD: Must store the last n transitions and wait n steps before updating. Memory: $O(n)$ per state. Update: one (s,a) pair per step.

Eligibility traces: Update all (s,a) pairs simultaneously in one pass. Memory: $O(|S||A|)$ for the trace table. No waiting — updates happen every step.

Key advantage: eligibility traces are online (update every step) and backward (credit all recent states). n-Step TD is forward-looking and requires buffering.
