🏢 Business Problem

Warsaw ASP — Direct Policy Optimisation

Instead of learning a Q-table and deriving a policy, Policy Gradient methods directly optimise $\pi(a|s,\theta) = \text{softmax}(\theta[s][a])$. No Q-table needed. This is the foundation for modern deep RL (A2C, PPO).

🧮 Policy Gradient Theorem

$$\nabla J(\theta) \propto \sum_s \mu(s) \sum_a Q^\pi(s,a) \nabla \pi(a|s,\theta)$$
Gradient of expected return w.r.t. policy parameters. $\nabla \log \pi(a|s) = (\mathbf{1}[a=A_t] - \pi(a|s)) / \tau$

REINFORCE

$$G_t = \sum_{k=t}^{T} \gamma^{k-t} R_{k+1}$$
$$\theta \leftarrow \theta + \alpha \gamma^t G_t \nabla \log \pi(A_t|S_t,\theta)$$
Monte Carlo: waits for full episode. Unbiased (uses actual return). High variance. Implemented in reinforce().

REINFORCE with Baseline

$$\theta \leftarrow \theta + \alpha \gamma^t (G_t - b(S_t)) \nabla \log \pi(A_t|S_t,\theta)$$
$b(s)$ does not introduce bias (baseline is state-dependent, not action-dependent). Reduces variance significantly. Recommended in practice.

Actor-Critic TD(0)

$$\delta_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma V(S') - V(S)$$
$$V(S) \leftarrow V(S) + \alpha_v \delta_t$$
$$\theta \leftarrow \theta + \alpha \gamma^t \delta_t \nabla \log \pi(A_t|S_t,\theta)$$
Online updates per step. TD critic reduces variance but introduces bias. Foundation for A2C and PPO. Implemented in actor_critic_td0().

Temperature τ

$$\pi(a|s,\theta) = \frac{\exp(\theta[s][a] / \tau)}{\sum_{a'} \exp(\theta[s][a'] / \tau)}$$
τ=1.0: standard. τ<1: sharper policy (more deterministic). τ>1: more uniform (more exploration).

Purpose: Shows how deterministic the policy becomes over time.
What to watch:
• High entropy = uniform policy (exploring)
• Low entropy = deterministic policy (exploiting)
• Healthy: gradual decrease. Sudden drop = policy collapsed.

Task 1 — Softmax policy

State S2, logits: $\theta[S2] = [2.0, 3.5, 1.0, 0.5]$, τ=1.0. Compute $\pi(A1|S2)$.

💡 Show answer
$$\pi(A1|S2) = \frac{e^{3.5}}{e^{2.0} + e^{3.5} + e^{1.0} + e^{0.5}} = \frac{33.11}{7.39 + 33.11 + 2.72 + 1.65} = \frac{33.11}{44.87} \approx \mathbf{0.738}$$

Task 2 — REINFORCE gradient update

At step t=2 in an episode: $G_2=12$, $\pi(A1|S3)=0.6$, $\gamma=0.95$, $\alpha=0.01$. Compute $\Delta\theta[S3][A1]$ (the gradient update for the chosen action A1).

💡 Show answer
$\nabla \log \pi(A1|S3) = 1 - \pi(A1|S3) = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4$ (for the chosen action)
$$\Delta\theta[S3][A1] = \alpha \cdot \gamma^t \cdot G_t \cdot \nabla \log \pi = 0.01 \cdot 0.95^2 \cdot 12 \cdot 0.4$$
$$= 0.01 \cdot 0.9025 \cdot 12 \cdot 0.4 = \mathbf{0.0433}$$

Task 3 — Baseline variance reduction

REINFORCE returns over 5 episodes: [5, 15, 8, 20, 12]. Baseline b(s)=12. Compute the advantage $(G_t - b)$ for each episode. Does the baseline reduce variance?

💡 Show answer
Advantages: $[5-12, 15-12, 8-12, 20-12, 12-12] = [-7, 3, -4, 8, 0]$

Variance of returns: $\text{Var}([5,15,8,20,12]) = 30.0$
Variance of advantages: $\text{Var}([-7,3,-4,8,0]) = 30.0$

Variance is the same! The baseline reduces variance of the gradient estimator, not the returns themselves. The key is that $b(s)$ is state-dependent — it removes the "average" value, leaving only the advantage signal.

Task 4 — Actor-Critic vs REINFORCE

Why does Actor-Critic TD(0) have lower variance than REINFORCE, even though it is biased?

💡 Show answer
REINFORCE variance: Uses full episode return $G_t$ which depends on all future random transitions. High variance, especially for long episodes.

Actor-Critic variance: Uses TD error $\delta_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma V(S') - V(S)$ which only looks one step ahead. Much lower variance.

Bias trade-off: Actor-Critic is biased because $V(S')$ is an estimate (not the true value). But in practice, lower variance often outweighs the bias — especially early in training when the policy is far from optimal.

Task 5 — Why policy gradient for deep RL

Q-Learning works well for the tabular ASP. Why would you use Policy Gradient methods for a larger, continuous-state version of the same problem?

💡 Show answer
Q-Learning limitations at scale:
• Q-table size = $|S| \times |A|$ — infeasible for continuous states
• Cannot represent stochastic policies (always greedy)
• Cannot handle continuous action spaces

Policy Gradient advantages:
• Policy $\pi(a|s,\theta)$ can be a neural network — scales to any state space
• Naturally represents stochastic policies
• Works with continuous actions (Gaussian policy)
• Foundation for A2C, PPO, SAC — state-of-the-art deep RL
